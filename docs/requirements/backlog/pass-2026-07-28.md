## Minted from the NFR pass

Three requirements the NFR document (weight 20) demanded and the backlog did not
yet carry. Filed in the requirements source, per Perpetum 0.8:

- **`N-9`** — no panic on a runtime-fallible path; typed errors. Follows from
  NFR §3 and from `L-7`: a harness that panics mid-batch cannot reconcile.
- **`N-10`** — atomic writes (temp file + rename) for every derived file.
  Follows from NFR §4; without it a crash mid-write leaves a projection that
  contradicts the journal, which is exactly the failure `L-4` exists to prevent.
- **`N-11`** — dependency policy: std-first, reason recorded at the point of
  addition, warm-cache offline build.

## Shape of the work

The dependency structure is stark and decides Phase C's batching more than the
scores do:

- **Everything depends on the journal** (`L-3`). State projection, recovery,
  rewind, cost accounting, the board, `/explain` — all of it reads or writes the
  same record stream.
- **Nothing depends on the model layer.** `M-*` is the largest cluster and the
  most quotable, but the loop's spine — steps, journal, gates, evidence — is
  testable with no model at all, and must be, or the tests need a GPU to run.
- **`V-2` and `V-3` are cheap early and expensive late.** The gate runner has to
  keep transcripts from its first line of code; retrofitting evidence onto a
  runner that returns a boolean means rewriting every caller.
